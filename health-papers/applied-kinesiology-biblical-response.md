## TL;DR (For the Reader in a Hurry)

- Applied Kinesiology (AK) is a muscle-testing diagnostic system invented in 1964 by Detroit chiropractor George J. Goodheart, Jr., and now widely practiced by chiropractors, naturopaths, integrative MDs, and lay practitioners under dozens of names (Touch for Health, Contact Reflex Analysis, Neuro Emotional Technique, NAET, BodyTalk, PSYCH-K, ART, and more).[^1]
- Blinded peer-reviewed studies repeatedly show AK performs no better than chance for the substance-identification claims it makes[^2] — yet many patients and practitioners report uncanny accuracy, and that experience is real and must be taken seriously, not dismissed.
- Scripture and the Spirit of Prophecy teach that real spiritual powers operate behind divinatory methods (Deut 18:9–14; 1 Sam 28; Acts 16:16–18). Accurate divination is more spiritually dangerous than inaccurate divination, not less. The gospel of Jesus Christ — Creator, Healer, Redeemer of the whole person — is the true wholistic answer the seeker is looking for.

## 1. Why People Are Drawn to AK — Listen Before You Speak

Before we critique anything, we must love. The neighbor who walks into a chiropractor’s office and submits to muscle testing — or the church member who quietly orders supplements based on a kinesiology reading — is almost never chasing the occult. He or she is chasing something good.

People turn to AK for reasons that are deeply legitimate and even God-given:

- **Chronic illness that mainstream medicine has not solved.** A patient with fibromyalgia, long-COVID fatigue, gut dysbiosis, autoimmunity, or undiagnosed pain has often been told, “Your labs are normal; here’s an antidepressant.” They are not crazy and they are not stupid. They are suffering, and someone finally listens.
- **The longing to be treated as a whole person.** Fifteen-minute appointments, EMR-driven note-taking, and the carving of the body into specialist silos leave patients feeling like a collection of parts. AK practitioners typically spend an hour with a patient and treat body-mind-spirit as connected. That longing — to be seen — is a longing God put there.
- **Personalized care.** “What is right for *your* body?” is a beautiful question. The Adventist health message answers it. AK answers it falsely.
- **The intuition that body and spirit are linked.** Scripture teaches the same thing (1 Thess 5:23; 3 John 2). The intuition is correct; the system being used to act on it is not.

Our first move with a seeker drawn to AK is to validate these longings as God-given, not to mock them. *“You are right to want wholeness. You are right that you are more than your lab values. You are right that healing involves more than a pill.”* From there we can speak.

## 2. What AK Actually Is

**Origin.** Applied Kinesiology was developed in 1964 by George J. Goodheart, Jr., D.C. (1918–2008), a Detroit-area chiropractor practicing in Grosse Pointe, Michigan.[^3] Goodheart claimed he restored a patient’s chronic winged scapula by pressing nodules near the origin and insertion of the serratus anterior muscle, and from that beginning developed what became AK. The International College of Applied Kinesiology (ICAK) was founded in the mid-1970s.[^4]

**The “triad of health.”** ICAK teaches that health rests on a triangle of structural, chemical, and mental factors, and that imbalance in any of the three will manifest as muscle weakness on testing.[^5]

**Manual muscle testing (MMT).** The practitioner applies pressure to an outstretched arm or another muscle group while the patient resists. A “strong” muscle is interpreted as a healthy organ, a tolerated food, or a “yes.” A “weak” muscle (one that gives way under pressure) is interpreted as a diseased organ, an offending substance, or a “no.” Substances are typically placed in the patient’s hand, on the tongue, or in the lap; questions may be held silently in the patient’s mind.

**An important distinction.** Manual muscle testing as developed by Kendall and Kendall (1949) for polio rehabilitation is a legitimate part of physical therapy and neurology — it is the careful, standardized assessment of muscle strength grades 0–5. Academic kinesiology, the science of human movement, is a respectable university discipline. **Applied Kinesiology is something different**: it uses the *appearance* of a muscle test as a divinatory or diagnostic indicator about organs, foods, allergies, emotions, and events the muscle cannot mechanically know about.[^6]

**The family of derivatives.** Within a decade of Goodheart’s first work, AK had branched into a sprawling family of lay and professional systems. Among the most influential:

- **Touch for Health** (John F. Thie, D.C., book published 1973) — the lay popularization of AK, taught in over 100 countries.[^7] The publisher DeVorss describes the 50th Anniversary Edition (2023) as “the fundamental text of Energy Kinesiology for balancing muscles, posture, and ‘Chi’ (Life Energy)” with “over a million copies in print since 1973.”[^8]
- **Behavioral Kinesiology** (John Diamond, M.D., 1979) — integrates AK with thymus energy, music vibrations, and “life energy” of the meridians.
- **Contact Reflex Analysis (CRA)** — Dick A. Versendaal, D.C. (1937–2014); CRA Wellness describes it as “an energy connection… utilizing a muscle response as an indicator and assessment of the energy status of each organ, gland, system, or structure.”[^9]
- **Total Body Modification (TBM); Neuro Emotional Technique (NET); BodyTalk; NAET (Nambudripad’s Allergy Elimination Techniques); PSYCH-K; ART (Autonomic Response Testing); Health Kinesiology; Quantum Neurology.**[^10]
- **“Power vs. Force”** (David R. Hawkins, M.D., 1995), which uses AK muscle testing to “calibrate” levels of consciousness, truth, world religions, historical figures, and books on a 1–1000 scale of “vibration.”[^11] Hawkins’ work is openly framed within Hindu and New Age cosmology.

**This is not fringe.** ICAK has chapters on every inhabited continent, and the Touch for Health lay movement has spread across more than 100 countries.[^12]

## 3. The Spiritual Lineage — Why AK Cannot Be Separated From Its Roots

A defender of AK will say, “It’s just biomechanics — there’s no religion in it.” That claim does not survive a serious look at the literature.

**Chiropractic’s vitalistic foundation.** AK was born inside the chiropractic profession, and chiropractic was born inside American spiritualism. Daniel David Palmer (1845–1913), the founder of chiropractic, was a self-described Spiritualist and magnetic healer before he was a chiropractor.[^13] In his 1914 memoir he wrote: *“The knowledge and philosophy given me by Dr. Jim Atkinson, an intelligent spiritual being… appealed to my reason. The method by which I obtained an explanation of certain physical phenomena, from an intelligence in the spiritual world, is known in biblical language as inspiration.”*[^14] Palmer’s “Innate Intelligence” was explicitly defined as “a segment of that Intelligence which fills the universe” — a pantheistic universal soul, not a Christian doctrine.[^15]

This does not mean every chiropractic adjustment is occult, nor that every chiropractor is a spiritualist; many today are Bible-believing Christians who practice straightforward musculoskeletal therapy. But it does mean the *philosophical womb* from which AK emerged was already spiritualistic.

**Goodheart and the meridians.** A peer-reviewed history of AK published in the *Journal of Manipulative and Physiological Therapeutics* (Gin & Green, 1997) states plainly: *“Later, influenced by the writings of Felix Mann, M.D., Goodheart incorporated acupuncture meridian therapy into the AK system.”*[^16] Felix Mann’s *Acupuncture: The Ancient Chinese Art of Healing* (1962) introduced traditional Chinese chi (life-energy) theory to the English-speaking medical world.[^17] Goodheart took that chi theory, married it to muscle testing, and produced a hybrid in which the muscle was now a feedback gauge of “energy flow.” Without chi, the entire interpretive framework of AK collapses; you are left with arm-strength measurements that mean nothing.

**Touch for Health goes openly New Age.** John Thie, the founding chairman of ICAK, stripped out the professional gatekeeping and taught lay people to “balance” their family’s Chi. The DeVorss publisher copy describes Touch for Health as having launched “the emerging profession of Energy Kinesiology, Energy Medicine and Energy Psychology.”[^18]

**Diamond, Hawkins, and “consciousness.”** John Diamond explicitly framed AK as a tool to “ask the body” and receive answers from “the unconscious.” David Hawkins took AK further and used it to determine the “vibrational level” of Jesus, Buddha, Krishna, and historical figures, and to test the truth-value of statements — a textbook divination practice with a muscle test in place of a pendulum.[^19]

**The honest admission of practitioners.** Many AK practitioners do not hide this. The Christian Research Institute summarizes the family of practices: *“Energetic medicine encompasses dozens of diverse therapies and diagnostic approaches, including meridian therapy (e.g., acupuncture, acupressure), Applied Kinesiology… homeopathy, reflexology.”*[^20] And the same article notes: *“Wherever it has appeared — in ancient paganism, modern occultism, or parapsychological research — this ‘life force’ has been accompanied by altered states of consciousness, psychic phenomena, and contact with spirits.”*[^21]

You cannot detach a diagnostic method from the cosmology that tells you what its results mean. If the muscle is reading “energy,” you are inside a worldview foreign to Scripture.

## 4. The Evidence — Briefly, and in Its Proper Place

Blinded studies of AK go back four decades, and the pattern is consistent. When the practitioner cannot see, smell, or otherwise sense what substance is being tested, AK collapses to the level of guessing:

- **Triano (1982)** — muscle strength testing as a screen for nutritional therapy: blind testing failed.[^22]
- **Kenney et al. (1988)** — three experienced AK practitioners; the study concluded “the use of applied kinesiology to evaluate nutrient status is no more useful than random guessing.”[^23]
- **Garrow (1988)** — kinesiology and food allergy: no validity demonstrated.[^24]
- **Lüdtke et al. (2001)** — test-retest reliability and validity of the kinesiology muscle test: poor.[^25]
- **Pothmann et al. (2001)** — evaluation of AK in childhood nutritional intolerance: poor agreement.[^26]
- **Hall, Lewith, Brien & Little (2008)** — literature review concluding the available AK studies do not meet basic standards of diagnostic accuracy.[^27]
- **Schwartz, Utts, Spottiswoode et al. (2014)** — a sympathetic, double-blind, randomized study using poison vs. saline vials concluded: “Applied Kinesiology has not demonstrated that it is a useful or reliable diagnostic tool upon which health decisions can be based.”[^28]

What does this evidence actually mean? It rules out the *materialist* mechanism AK theory proposes: the substance is not, in fact, transmitting information to the body through any biomechanical pathway the muscle can sense. **But it does not rule out that some other mechanism is in play.** And here secular skepticism stops, while Christian discernment is just beginning.

## 5. The Spiritual Reality Behind AK — The Heart of the Matter

If naturalistic explanations fully accounted for AK, blinded studies would be the end of the conversation. They are not, because something else has to be explained: practitioners and patients regularly report *“It worked. It found my Lyme. It told me which supplement. It identified the trauma I’d never told anyone.”*

Three categories of explanation are on the table.

**(a) Natural.** Placebo, regression to the mean, therapeutic alliance, ideomotor effect, practitioner cueing (conscious or unconscious), and confirmation bias all genuinely operate. The ideomotor phenomenon — first named by William Carpenter in 1852 — explains how Ouija board planchettes, dowsing rods, and pendulums move: tiny unconscious muscle movements amplified by a lever.[^29] AK functionally operates on the same principle: a question is asked, the practitioner subtly varies pressure or expectation, and the patient’s arm “answers.” This is real and explains much.

**(b) Deceptive but non-spiritual.** Some AK results come from sloppy cold-reading, sales motives (the practitioner happens to stock the supplement the muscle “needs”), and unconscious manipulation of the test.

**(c) Genuinely spiritual.** Scripture forbids us to close this category. A diagnostic ritual that asks the body yes/no questions about hidden information — past traumas, in utero memories, the right remedy, the offending allergen, future outcomes — is structurally identical to other practices Scripture names as divination. And Scripture insists those practices are not always *empty*.

Consider the texts:

> *“There shall not be found among you any one… that useth divination, or an observer of times, or an enchanter, or a witch, or a charmer, or a consulter with familiar spirits, or a wizard, or a necromancer. For all that do these things are an abomination unto the LORD.”* — Deuteronomy 18:10–12 (KJV)

God’s prohibition is total — and the reason given is not that the practices are *ineffective*. The reason is that they are *an abomination*, because they access reality through unauthorized channels.

In 1 Samuel 28, King Saul visits the medium at Endor. Real contact occurs. Samuel — or something speaking as Samuel — appears, delivers an accurate prophecy of Saul’s death the next day, and Saul dies as foretold. The accuracy did not redeem the act; it sealed his judgment.

The decisive New Testament text is Acts 16:16–18. A slave girl in Philippi follows Paul, crying out, *“These men are the servants of the most high God, which shew unto us the way of salvation.”* Every word she said was true. Yet Luke describes her with the Greek phrase *pneuma pythōna* — “a python spirit” — a reference to the oracular serpent of Delphi. Paul did not say, “She’s accurate, she’s on our side.” Paul was grieved, turned, and commanded the spirit out of her in the name of Jesus Christ. **The accurate words were the proof the spirit had to go, not the proof it could stay.**

This is the Acts 16 principle, and it is the master key to AK: ***accurate divination is more spiritually dangerous than inaccurate divination, not less, because deception that confirms itself is the hardest deception to escape.***

- **2 Corinthians 11:13–15** — Satan is “transformed into an angel of light,” and his ministers as ministers of righteousness.
- **1 Timothy 4:1** — in the last days some will depart from the faith, “giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils.”
- **1 Corinthians 10:20–21** — *“Ye cannot drink the cup of the Lord, and the cup of devils.”*
- **Galatians 5:19–20** — among the works of the flesh, *pharmakeia* (sorcery) is listed alongside idolatry.
- **Exodus 22:18; Leviticus 19:31; 20:6, 27** — the Old Testament treats divination, mediumship, and sorcery as capital offenses; the gravity is theological, not merely cultural.
- **Revelation 18:23** — Babylon falls because *“by thy sorceries [pharmakeia] were all nations deceived.”*

**Ellen White’s testimony confirms the same line.** She wrote of the last days:

> *“Through the agency of spiritualism, miracles will be wrought, the sick will be healed, and many undeniable wonders will be performed. And as the spirits will profess faith in the Bible, and manifest respect for the institutions of the church, their work will be accepted as a manifestation of divine power.”*[^30]

And on the very next page:

This is not Ellen White’s stray opinion. It is one of the central warnings of *The Great Controversy* chapters 31–34, and it is the lens through which Adventists are commissioned to read every “energy healing” trend.

## 6. Particularly Dangerous Applications

Not every form of AK carries equal risk. The closer the practice moves to overt divination, the more urgent the warning.

- **Surrogate testing**, in which one person’s muscle is tested in place of another’s (often a child, animal, or absent person) — this is functionally identical to spirit-medium proxy work. Matthew 18:6 sounds a sober warning against causing little ones to stumble.
- **Distance/remote testing** — testing for someone who is not physically present, sometimes only with a photograph or a name. This is non-local information transfer with no physical mechanism; biblically it is divination.
- **“Emotional clearance” sessions** — finding hidden traumas, “ancestral wounds,” “generational curses,” past-life memories, in-utero memories. Whatever the practitioner’s intent, the patient is being told an authoritative narrative about their own hidden history that no human or muscle can know. This frequently produces false memories and real spiritual harm.
- **Diagnostic claims** for serious conditions — Lyme disease, parasites, “candida overgrowth,” heavy metal toxicity, mold, hidden cancer. Patients have delayed real medical care on the strength of muscle tests. The American Cancer Society has stated “available scientific evidence does not support the claim that applied kinesiology can diagnose or treat cancer or other illness.”[^39]
- **The supplement-sales loop** — where the practitioner profits from the same products the muscle test “selects.” This is a structural conflict of interest the patient is rarely told about.
- **Quasi-pastoral roles** — practitioners who “clear” emotions, declare spiritual diagnoses, “test” the influence of dead relatives, or pray over substances. They are functioning as priests of an unauthorized religion.

## 7. The Gospel as the True Wholistic Answer

We are not here merely to warn people away from something. We are here to invite them into Someone.

**Christ is the Creator of body, mind, and soul.** *“And the very God of peace sanctify you wholly; and I pray God your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless unto the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ”* (1 Thess 5:23). Wholistic anthropology did not originate with naturopathy or with Eastern medicine. It originated in Genesis 2:7, where the LORD God formed man of the dust and breathed into him the breath of life.

**Christ is the Great Physician who heals personally.** *“They that be whole need not a physician, but they that are sick”* (Matt 9:12). Jesus heals by name, by touch, by word — never by an impersonal force. He knows the woman with the issue of blood (Mark 5); He calls Lazarus by name (John 11); He weeps. An impersonal “energy” cannot know you, cannot love you, cannot redeem you. Only a personal Creator can do those things, and He has.

**The gospel is good news for the whole person.** Forgiveness of sin (1 John 1:9), peace with God (Rom 5:1), a renewed mind (Rom 12:2), a body destined for resurrection (1 Cor 15), restored relationships (2 Cor 5:18), transformed habits (Titus 2:11–12), and a hope that does not put us to shame (Rom 5:5).

**The Adventist health message.** Ellen White wrote:

> *“Pure air, sunlight, abstemiousness, rest, exercise, proper diet, the use of water, trust in divine power — these are the true remedies.”*[^40]

Eight simple, freely available means of grace. Adventist Health Study-2 (AHS-2), which recruited 96,469 Seventh-day Adventist men and women between 2002 and 2007, has documented in mainstream peer-reviewed journals that vegetarian dietary patterns are associated with reduced all-cause mortality and reduced incidence of diabetes, hypertension, metabolic syndrome, and certain cancers.[^41] A review of initial AHS-2 findings confirmed significant protective associations across multiple chronic disease categories.[^42] In the earlier Adventist Health Study-1 (AHS-1), Fraser and Shavlik documented that Adventist vegetarian men lived on average 9.5 years longer, and women 6.1 years longer, than the comparable California population.[^43] Loma Linda was identified by Dan Buettner in his November 2005 *National Geographic* cover story as the only U.S. “Blue Zone” — a community of verifiable, documented longevity rooted in a biblical lifestyle.[^44]

This is the answer to the legitimate longing AK exploits. *God’s way actually works*, in the most ordinary, peer-reviewable, lab-confirmable terms.

**Prayer and anointing.** *“Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church; and let them pray over him, anointing him with oil in the name of the Lord. And the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up”* (James 5:14–15). This is the Bible’s authorized model for spiritual involvement in healing — personal, covenantal, prayerful, submitted to God’s will. It is the polar opposite of an impersonal energy diagnosis.

**The Holy Spirit, not an impersonal force.** The third Person of the Godhead is not chi, prana, “life energy,” or “the body’s wisdom.” He is a Person who indwells believers, convicts of sin, glorifies Christ, and is the actual source of life and healing (John 16:13–14; Rom 8:11).

## 8. Bridge Conversations — How to Talk With a Seeker Drawn to AK

The goal is never to win an argument. The goal is to walk a person from the felt need AK seemed to meet, to the Person who actually meets it.

**Validate the felt need.**

> *“It sounds like you’ve been through a lot, and conventional medicine hasn’t had answers. I want to take that seriously. Tell me more about what you’re hoping for.”*

**Take their report seriously.**

> *“I’m not going to tell you that what you experienced wasn’t real. I actually think something real did happen. Can I tell you what the Bible says about why some of these methods do produce real effects — and why that’s actually the most important reason to be careful?”*

**Common ground first.**

> *“You’re right that mainstream medicine often treats people like a collection of parts. You’re right that body, mind, and spirit are connected. Let me tell you about a wholistic approach that goes even further — because it includes who you are eternally, and it’s been quietly producing the longest-lived community in America for over a century.”*

**Ask the diagnostic questions.**

> *“What is the ‘energy’ you’re being told to align with? Where does it come from? What is its character? Does it know your name? Does it love you? Can it forgive you? Can it raise you from the dead?”*

> *“Who taught the practitioner this method? Who taught their teacher? Whose worldview is it built on?”*

**Introduce Acts 16 winsomely.**

> *“Can I show you a story from the Bible that surprised me? There’s a girl in Acts 16 who was telling the truth about Paul — accurately! — and Paul still cast the spirit out of her. Why would he do that? Because the source mattered more than the accuracy. The Bible takes accurate fortune-telling more seriously than inaccurate fortune-telling, not less.”*

**Respond to “but it’s just science.”**

> *“I love science — I love it enough to want to follow it where it actually leads. When AK has been tested under blinded conditions — beginning with Triano in 1982 and continuing through Schwartz in 2014 — it performs no better than chance. So if it’s not transmitting biological information, but people are still reporting accurate results, we have to ask what is producing those results. The Bible has an explanation; secular science doesn’t.”*

**Don’t condemn. Invite.**

> *“I’m not asking you to be afraid. I’m asking you to consider that there’s a healer who knows your name, who has paid for every sin, who is the source of every good thing — and who has given His church a beautifully ordinary way of healing: rest, food, water, sunlight, fresh air, trust, prayer, and the laying on of hands by elders. May I show you?”*

## 9. For Those Coming Out of AK and Energy Work — A Pastoral Path

Some readers will recognize themselves in this handout. They have practiced AK on themselves or their children. They have submitted to surrogate testing. They have books, pendants, and supplement cabinets full of muscle-tested choices. They feel uneasy now, and they should not feel condemned.

**The good news first.** *“If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness”* (1 John 1:9). No involvement is too deep for the blood of Jesus.

**Concrete steps:**

1. **Confess and renounce.** Name the specific practices before God. Renounce any “openings” given knowingly or unknowingly to spirits other than the Holy Spirit. The Ephesian believers of Acts 19:18–19 *“came, and confessed, and shewed their deeds. Many of them also which used curious arts brought their books together, and burned them before all men.”* Disposing of testing-related books, charts, pendulums, divination cards, and dubious literature is biblical, not extreme.
1. **Seek prayer ministry.** Ask trusted pastors and praying believers to pray over you, anointing you in the name of Jesus per James 5:14–15. In cases of deeper involvement, deliverance prayer led by mature, biblically grounded ministers may be appropriate.
1. **Reclaim the mind.** Saturate the mind with Scripture. Memorize Psalm 91, Romans 8, Ephesians 6. *“Whatsoever things are true… think on these things”* (Phil 4:8).
1. **Reassess products.** Supplements selected through muscle testing are not magic — but neither are they vetted. Get a real medical assessment of what your body actually needs. Discard products you cannot account for.
1. **Reconnect with the body of Christ.** Spiritual deception thrives in isolation. Local church family, Sabbath worship, communion, and shared prayer are means of grace God has given for exactly this kind of healing.
1. **Be patient.** Coming out can stir up grief, fear, even physical symptoms as old habits are replaced. The Lord is gentle. He who began a good work will finish it (Phil 1:6).

## 10. Practical Discernment Going Forward

**Red flags in any health practice:**

- The practitioner “tests” for things the muscle (or any physical instrument) has no way to know — emotions, past traumas, ancestral influences, future outcomes, hidden organs, allergies to substances in a sealed bottle.
- “Energy” is invoked as the explanatory mechanism, undefined or defined as chi/prana/“life force.”
- The practice has roots in Eastern religious cosmology, Spiritualism, New Age teaching, or “channeled” sources.
- The practitioner uses pendulums, dowsing rods, “intuition,” or claims of divine guidance to diagnose.
- Strong sales pressure linked to in-house supplements.
- Practitioner takes on spiritual or pastoral roles in your life.
- You feel mentally foggy, passive, or “different” after sessions.

**Questions worth asking:**

- *“What is the underlying theory by which this is supposed to work?”*
- *“Has this method been tested under blinded conditions?”*
- *“What was the spiritual orientation of the founder?”*
- *“Are you, the practitioner, doing this in the name of Jesus Christ — and if so, by what biblical authority?”*

**When to flee versus when to engage.** Flee any practice that requires you to be passive, surrender your will, or “ask the body” questions about hidden information. Engage your physician, your nutritionist, your physical therapist, your Christian counselor, your pastor. Engage the eight natural remedies daily. Engage in believing prayer.

**Pray protection over self and household.** *“Submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you”* (James 4:7). The blood of Jesus, the name of Jesus, the Word of God, and the fellowship of the saints are the standing armor of the believer (Eph 6:10–18).
